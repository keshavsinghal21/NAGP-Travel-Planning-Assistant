# Essential Travel Information

## Weather

Enjoy summer all year-round with Singapore tropical climate (25-33ºC). Dress in light, airy clothing and bring along an umbrella in case of occasional rainfall.\u0026nbsp;Singapore Standard Time is situated in GMT +8 time zone, so be sure to calibrate your watches accordingly.


## Languages

You can communicate in English with Singaporeans, who are mostly fluent in it. Many also speak Mandarin Chinese, Malay or Tamil. You may also encounter Singlish, a unique colloquial form of English that blends with various languages and dialects spoken in Singapore


## Power Plug

The standard electrical current used in Singapore is 220-240 volts AC (50 cycles) and you can use power plugs with three square prongs here.

## Drinking Water

Tap water in Singapore surpasses World Health Organization standards. You can drink water straight from the source or easily purchase them from stores.

## Currency

The official currency used in Singapore is the Singapore dollar (SGD).

Credit cards are widely accepted in many restaurants, shops, hotels and taxis. ATMs are also available around Singapore.


## Smoking

Smoking is prohibited in buildings, retail food establishments, transport nodes, public service vehicles, and sports facilities. Designated smoking areas are marked with notices or signs.

## TAX Refund

Tourists can claim a refund on the 9% Goods and Services Tax (GST) paid on your purchases if you spend more than $100 at participating shops.

## Visa and Entry Information

Visa requirements depend on the traveller's nationality and other circumstances.

Travellers should check the latest requirements with the Immigration & Checkpoints Authority (ICA) or the relevant Singapore mission before travelling.

The Singapore Arrival Card (SGAC) may also be required before arrival.

## Practical Tips

Singapore's hot and humid weather should be considered when planning outdoor sightseeing.

Public transport is useful for travelling between different parts of the city.

Visitors should also follow local rules and respect public places and cultural sites.
